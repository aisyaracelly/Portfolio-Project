## Full Transcript

If I was starting a brand new B2B content strategy tomorrow, knowing what I know after 4 years on LinkedIn, over 170,000 followers, and $2 million in content-driven revenue, I would not build a personal brand. Or at least, not the way most people are telling you to. I would build something different. Something that almost no B2B company is doing right. But the ones who are doing it are pulling away from everyone else in their market.

So in the next 10 minutes, I will show you exactly what I would build, why it works, and the five-step playbook to install it in your company before the end of this quarter.

All right, first of all, here is why I would do it differently this time for new content strategy. The LinkedIn game has changed. In 2022, one person posting consistently was enough to win. But in 2026, the competition is much harder. So right now, just posting as a founder isn't enough anymore. The feed is saturated, AI content is everywhere, and personal brands are hitting a wall, even the ones doing everything right. It's just that there's too much competition.

The companies pulling away aren't the ones with the loudest single voice. They're the ones with three, four, or even five voices coming from the same company, all coordinated, all hitting their own audience, all stacking the same brand signal at the same time. It's called team-led content, and it's the single best B2B LinkedIn strategy right now.

Here is exactly how I would build it. I will give you my five-step playbook to build your team-led content strategy.

**Step 1: Pick Your Starting Team**

I would start with three to five people for the pilot, not the whole company, obviously, not just the founder, either. I would pick them on three different criteria:

- Criteria 1: Subject matter expertise. They know their domain deeply enough to teach it.
- Criteria 2: Client-facing roles like sales, product, customer success, or leadership. Someone who actually talks to the market every week.
- Criteria 3: Willingness to be visible. They want to do this. Not "Fine, I will try." They are incentivized to do this.

For most B2B companies, the ideal starting lineup is the founder, one subject matter expert like a VP of product, and one revenue leader like the head of growth. On top of that, you can add the CFO and the COO.

**Step 2: Give Each Person a Content Territory**

This is where most companies blow it. They tell three people to post on LinkedIn and they end up with random, overlapping content that confuses the audience and exhausts the team. Each person needs a clear lane. I call it a topical map.

You need three topics per person and one personal brand angle per person — four topics total, including the personal brand. Each topic has subtopics inside and very little overlap with other team members.

Here is what it looks like for B2B SaaS:
- The CEO posts about market trends, leadership, and company building.
- The VP of product posts about product strategy, user research, and roadmap decisions.
- The head of growth posts about demand gen, conversion, and LinkedIn tactics.

Same company, same audience, three distinct angles. Each topic has a direct connection with your company offer. When you map it right, you get 60+ content ideas before you publish a single post.

**Step 3: Match the Content Level to the Right Person**

Your B2B buyers aren't one persona. They are C-levels, managers, and individual contributors. Each engages with a completely different content level.

- Strategic content (high-level insights, market trends, vision, point of view) → C-levels and investors read this. Your CEO posts it.
- Tactical content (frameworks, playbooks, workflows) → Managers and senior contributors who need this to manage their teams. Your VPs post it.
- Operational content (tutorials, templates, checklists) → The people doing the work day-to-day save this. Your team experts post it.

When you match person to content level, your brand speaks to every layer of your buyers' organization at the same time. That's a coverage no single voice can replicate.

**Step 4: Run It Like a System, Not a Vibe**

If you skip the operating system, this whole strategy collapses in two weeks. You cannot rely on motivation.

Minimum viable system:
- One shared content calendar (Notion, Google Sheets, or whatever you already use) with three columns: who's posting, what format, when.
- A weekly sync to review what performed best, plan the next week, align on topics, review upcoming posts, and unblock people who are stuck.
- One central content support team — copywriters and designers who handle ideation, design, copywriting, editing, and scheduling. Your team members bring the ideas but don't fight inside Canva at 11pm.

Best cadence: three posts per week per person, staggered across days. CEO: Monday, Wednesday, Friday. VP Product: Tuesday, Thursday. Head of Growth: Monday, Wednesday, Friday. By end of week, your brand has touched the feed seven times instead of three.

**Step 5: Stack the Three Effects That Compound**

Team-led content triggers three effects a single profile cannot produce:

1. The "They Are Everywhere" Effect — Your prospect sees your CEO on Monday, your product lead on Wednesday, your growth lead on Friday. Same logo, three different voices. They feel like you are the dominant player in the space.

2. The "They Really Help Us" Effect — Three different angles cover different pain points at strategic, tactical, and operational levels. Your audience starts associating your brand with something genuinely helpful, increasing the chance they contact you.

3. The "They Are The Professionals" Effect — When multiple experts under one company name are posting, it builds a halo effect. "Their content is so good, they must be the pros. Their offer must be good too." The personal brands lift the company brand.

**Recap:**
1. Pick 3-5 people — start from a pilot
2. Give each person a clear content territory with minimal overlap
3. Match content level (strategic, tactical, operational) to the right person
4. Run it like a system — one calendar, one weekly sync, one content support factory
5. Let the three effects compound: omnipresence, helpfulness, and halo effect
