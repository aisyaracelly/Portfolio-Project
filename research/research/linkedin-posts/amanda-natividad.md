## Post 1 — When Google Stops Sending Clicks
*Posted: 15 hours ago*
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/amandanat_when-google-stops-sending-clicks-what-still-activity-7470490713688850432-RXwC

68% of Google searches now end without a click... to *anywhere.*

That finding comes from my co-author Rand Fishkin’s latest research, and yes, it’s a doozy. But before you spiral into "is SEO dead?" — don't.

Because the click was never the point. It was just the thing we could measure.

For years, we treated traffic like the scoreboard. More sessions meant the strategy was working. Less traffic meant something was broken.

But if Google, AI Overviews, Amazon, social feeds, and AI tools are increasingly filtering, ranking, summarizing, and recommending before a person ever lands on your site, then the job changes.

You don’t just need to "get the click."

You need to become the brand people and machines look for by name.

I wrote up what I’d actually do about it:

• who's still winning Google's clicks, and why it's not just "better content"
• why AI-referred visitors may be fewer but more valuable
• why branded search, owned assets, and correlation dashboards matter more than traffic charts
• the 3 bets I'd make that survive every algorithm update

I regret to inform you that clicks probably aren't coming back. But you can build marketing that still works when they don’t.

## Post 2 — AI Content vs Lived Experience
*Posted: 1 day ago*
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/amandanat_everyone-got-told-to-10x-their-content-with-activity-7470127608979120129-lj86

Everyone got told to 10x their content with AI.
 
So now every other post reads like it was steeped with the same lukewarm teabag. Same structure. Same “It’s not this. It’s that.” Same nothing.
 
They're no match for lived experience.
 
A large language model is a phenomenal line cook. Hand it a recipe that already exists online and it'll plate the dish faster and cleaner than you can. What it can't do is taste. 
 
It wasn't on that customer call this morning. It didn’t watch the feature flop and ruminate on why. It has no idea what it actually feels like to do the job — because it has never once done the job.
 
My friend Kaleigh Moore put it plainly on today's episode of Zero Click Marketing: the person who's hands-on with your product, talking to customers, running the experiments — that's a goldmine. Their point of view isn't published anywhere else. It's all firsthand.
 
Which means the most defensible content input you have is the people already on your payroll.
 
Full episode with Kaleigh is available wherever you get your podcasts.

## Post 3 — Winning on Emotion, Not Features
*Posted: 1 week ago*
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/amandanat_if-youre-winning-deals-on-features-and-pricing-activity-7467594632097476608-95QL

If you're winning deals on features and pricing, I have bad news.

You've already lost.

In most categories everyone has the same features at roughly the same price, and whatever edge you have gets copied by next quarter.

So once a buyer confirms you fit the budget and check the boxes, what makes them pick you over the other two contenders?

Not another spec.

It's whether your messaging makes them feel understood — whether you clearly know what they've tried before, why it let them down, and what's keeping them up at night.

That's the case Talia Wolf makes on this week's episode of Zero Click Marketing. She always describes a comparison page she rebuilt — making it longer, more detailed, more specific — that drove 54% more free trials.

Her bigger claim might be the one that sticks with you: B2B is more emotional than B2C.

## Post 4 — Zero Click Marketing Is Here
*Posted: 1 week ago*
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/amandanat_zero-click-marketing-is-here-like-it-or-activity-7462900724960550913-fvsr

Zero Click Marketing is here, like it or not.

Google is rolling out what it calls its biggest Search update in 25+ years: AI Mode, longer conversational queries, follow-up questions, agentic search, and custom-generated interfaces.

...a.k.a. fewer clean journeys, fewer obvious clicks, fewer attribution paths.

What this means for marketers:

We still need to be discoverable. We still need to create useful, credible, specific content. We still need to build a brand people recognize when our ideas get summarized, cited, mentioned, or repeated somewhere else.

The marketers who understand how to earn influence before the visit, outside the visit, and sometimes instead of the visit? They’ll be the ones with the advantage.

If you're new here to the zero-click world, welcome.
