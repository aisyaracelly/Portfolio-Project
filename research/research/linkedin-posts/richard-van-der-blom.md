## Post 2 — The Bakery Analogy (Bad Outreach)
*Posted: 22 hours ago*
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/richardvanderblom_this-morning-i-went-to-my-local-bakery-here-activity-7470379563026661379-wwIy

This morning I went to my local bakery here in Valencia. I tried to sell them my own bread. They were confused, looked at me like I was insane.

Yet every single week I receive multiple DM's doing exactly the same.
"Hi Richard, I enjoyed your recent post. Would you be interested in setting up a 30-min call on how we can improve your LinkedIn social selling initiatives?"

Three things are wrong with this.
1. You didn't read my post. You scanned it.
2. You have no idea what I need. You just know what you're selling.
3. You're pitching social selling services. To somebody who's been into social selling for 20 years

I always ask the same question: "Are you the guy that walks into a bakery selling his own bread?"
Funny enough. They never respond.

I can smell bad outreach from a mile distance.
→ Generic opener that could go to anyone
→ Immediate pitch before any connection
→ Zero research on who you're talking to
→ Selling a service you clearly don't use yourself

You want to know what works for me and my clients?
→ Reference something specific/ real insight from their content
→ Ask a genuine question before you pitch anything
→ Show you understand their world before offering yours
→ Lead with curiosity. Not a calendar link.

The difference between bad and good outreach takes 5 more minutes of your time. Lazy outreach treats people as targets. Good outreach treats people as humans. One gets ignored. The other starts conversations.

What's the worst outreach you've ever received?

## Post 5 — AI on LinkedIn: Who's Really to Blame
*Posted: 4 days ago*
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/richardvanderblom_car-accident-who-do-we-blame-the-car-no-activity-7468954262513889280-AKLF

Car accident. Who do we blame? The car? No. The driver. So why are we blaming AI for everything wrong on LinkedIn?

"LinkedIn lost all its value"
"Every post sounds the same"
"It's bots talking to bots"

People voting for an "AI slop" emoji to be added.... Really? Is that what we need? More people acting as a LinkedIn Cop?

It looks like the last week this debate exploded. And of course I did some digging.

80%+ of people complaining about AI ruining LinkedIn are native English speakers.
Meanwhile:
→ 70% of content creators are NOT native English speakers
→ 35% of those post in English as their default language
→ 99.9% of us were not born copywriters
→ 99.99% are not visual designers

I use AI. There, I'll say it openly, not ashamed at all. To fix grammar and to structure my thoughts. To brainstorm hooks and CTAs. Not to think for me, for now I can still manage that process myself.

So, don't blame me, blame the ones below:
1. Creator tools selling "we analyzed 1M viral posts so you can copy them." That's why your feed looks like a cloning factory.
2. Growth hackers pushing automated outreach scripts. That's why my inbox has 5 identical pitches today.
3. People outsourcing the entire process. "Write me a post about what a koala taught me about sales." Zero thought = Zero value, always has been. Always will be, with or without AI.
4. Engagement pods gaming every metric. 200 people liking each other's posts within 3 minutes. Some audience still falls for it, the algorithm sees through it. Eventually.
5. Comment-bait factories. "Type DRAGON for my secret playbook 🐉" flooding every feed with hollow interaction.

LinkedIn should really get a grip on this: Right now they don't distinguish between how AI was used. An expert with 20 years of knowledge, finally able to scale their insights with AI assistance? Treated the same as someone who typed "write me something viral" and hit post. One adds value while the other one pollutes.

Please judge the content, not the process or tool. The em-dash won't tell you if a post is worth reading. The insight will.

In case you hadn't noticed: I'm team "use AI wisely".
Are you team "AI should be banned from LinkedIn" or team "The more AI the better"?
