## Post 1 — Panic-led marketing
*Posted: 22 hours ago*
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/pierre-herubel-540b3949_panic-led-marketing-looks-like-this-try-activity-7470386406885023744-9rC5

Panic-led marketing looks like this:
- Try new tactics while the target audience is undefined
- Write new headlines with unclear messaging
- Blame tactics for not sending leads
- Jump on the latest AI hack

Businesses in this situation are always in a short term loop. As a result, they treat symptoms but never root problems. They focus on surface-level problems while deeper ones are hurting the business badly. They are changing the color of the wallpaper while the house is built on a ravine.

Sometimes, they even blame external factors:
- "Let's fire the agency, they send poor leads"
- "Our CRM doesn't have the right features"
- "This channel is bad, let's try a new one"

But deeper marketing problems are still here. And the causes are often the same:
- Too product-centric → "Our product is the best"
- The sunk cost fallacy → "We can't change this now"
- Not enough market-oriented → "The market will adapt"

As a result, product and strategic problems are silently killing the business.

Here's the perfect scenario to move on — analyze the 4 layers of marketing problems:
1. Surface-level Problems — You need to change your copy, channels, tactics, or tools to get more conversions. The criticality level is low, install a backlog and experiment changes.
2. Structural Problems — You need to plan structural changes with a 3-month roadmap. Audit your systems, teams, KPIs, and budget to revamp your marketing system.
3. Strategic Problems — You need to align your business and the market now. Work on your ICP, positioning, offer, and messaging. Each day that passes without addressing this issue, you accumulate marketing debt and diminish the perceived value.
4. Product Problems — You need to rethink your product capability for your target audience. It might be time to focus on a more specific use case or pivot your efforts to solve a stronger PURE problem (painful, urgent, recognized, expensive).

## Post 2 — The 95/5 Playbook
*Posted: 2 days ago*
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/pierre-herubel-540b3949_cmo-how-can-we-organize-our-marketing-in-activity-7469661396826324994-aCV2

CMO: "How can we organize our marketing in 2026?"
Me: "Start from step 1 of the 95/5 Playbook:"

Step 1 - Create 2 types of content for passive buyers
~95% of your target market is not in "buying mode". But most of them are always in "learning mode": they want to keep up to date with their industry and job requirements.

Type 1: Zero-click content makes your brand discoverable
- Thought-leadership (from the entire team is best)
- Podcasts and long form video content

Type 2: Gated content delivers deeper expertise
- Group webinars and private communities
- Industry reports or other long forms

99% of your content production volume is zero-click content as you need a constant flow for discovery. But the 1% gated content needs to be perfect.

Step 2 - Build a dynamic demand capture system
~5% are in "research or buying mode" but it doesn't necessarily mean they'll become inbound leads. They might prefer competitors or forget to contact you.

That's why you need those 2 tactics:
Tactic 1: Conversion content to turn audience into leads
- Case studies or white papers
- Solution demos to show the value proposition

Tactic 2: Warm Outbound to start conversations
- Signal-led emails and ABM
- Daily social selling

All your efforts need to lead to:
- An optimized website (true for SLG or PLG)
- An active sales process (not a passive order taker)
- A high-converting front end offer (build this one first)

Run this playbook for 6 months with proper iteration and you'll have a solid marketing system in place.

## Post 3 — Content Positioning Strategy
*Posted: 5 days ago*
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/pierre-herubel-540b3949_ive-helped-600-b2b-brands-build-their-content-activity-7468572369809272832-M0Qm

I've helped 600+ B2B brands build their content strategy.
Here's how I help them with content positioning:

First, I help them understand:
1. Why you need a "content positioning strategy"
You need to think your content like a product. It has competitors, a revenue model, and objectives, so it needs a positioning strategy. It's like building a media: Why should people listen to you instead of the dozens of other voices in your industry?

2. Content positioning impacts your content creation
When you're taking B2B content seriously, it's much more than "just post consistently". It impacts:
- Your tone of voice and content style (content posture)
- What you're talking about (high level POV, opinion, workflows...)
- The formats (case studies, infographics, carousels, screenshots...)

So:
3. You need the right positioning frameworks
4 frameworks for content positioning:
1. What content altitude? (strategic, tactical, operational)
2. What content complexity? Broad, accessible, specialized, niche
3. What content posture? Expert, Leader, Builder, Inspirer
4. What zone of messaging? Pillars 1, 2, and 3 create the zone

But if you're starting a brand or personal brand content strategy, it's better to intentionally work on those frameworks.

TL;DR: Work on your content positioning.

## Post 4 — 3 Rules of B2B Content
*Posted: 1 week ago*
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/pierre-herubel-540b3949_if-youre-serious-about-b2b-content-you-activity-7467849417958678528-ucqo

If you're serious about B2B content: You need to understand these 3 rules:
- Rule (1): Some content helps buyers discover you. Other content helps them buy from you. But you cannot control precisely which does what.
- Rule (2): You cannot predict which piece of content will trigger an "Aha! moment". Two buyers will react to 2 completely different pieces.
- Rule (3): A prospect can sit in "solution aware" for 6 months. Then read one case study and decide you're the right fit the next day.

These 3 rules converge into one core principle: You cannot precisely map which content moves each buyer to the next stage.

For example, you CAN'T predict this: "This podcast moves a CMO from problem aware to solution aware."

But there is good news. You can still control 6 crucial content parameters:
1. Precision of insights: make sure each piece of content provides helpful and accurate information for your customers.
2. Quality of content: invest in the right skills (strategy, copy, design, editing) to make the content great.
3. Coverage: ensure your content strategy covers every corner of what needs to be said (at the strategic, tactical, and operational level).
4. Volume of distribution: the more you publish, the more you increase your chances of being seen and trigger "Aha! moments."
5. Variety of formats: if you always publish the same format on the same channel, you will create format fatigue.
6. Iteration: always analyze the results of your content to update your plan, formats, topics, and overall strategy.

TLDR:
- You cannot control the buying journey
- But you can adapt your content to it

## Post 5 — External Voices in B2B Buying
*Posted: 1 week ago*
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/pierre-herubel-540b3949_ive-seen-b2b-marketing-change-a-lot-in-9-activity-7467487954098126849-lce9

I've seen B2B Marketing change a lot in 9 years.
The biggest change is this "buying decision driver":
External voices have a growing influence on buying committees.

Each member of the committee has their own source of insights.
- "I heard in this podcast that we should do this"
- "I follow this expert, they say we should not do it"
- "I participated in a webinar saying this tool is important"

They are influenced by external voices. Podcasts, communities, thought leaders, online trends. And they trust those sources more than sales reps.

Now what does it mean for your marketing strategy?
The answer is simple: You need to be one of those "external voices."
- You need to be active in the right communities
- You can have 5 team members publish expert content
- You should host the podcast they listen to (or get invited)

Understand where your audience gets insights from, and create content there.

In a nutshell, go from only being "the seller" to "the voice worth following."
